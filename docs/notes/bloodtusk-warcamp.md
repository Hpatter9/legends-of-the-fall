# Bloodtusk warcamp

Owner approved the [visual concept](../samples/bloodtusk-warcamp/concept.png), with scope limited to the orc map first. Work is on `feature/bloodtusk-warcamp` in `/workspace/legends-of-the-fall`. No merge, deployment or version bump.

Bloodtusk now has an asymmetric outer perimeter with clipped rocky shoulders, an offset irregular inner curtain and repositioned towers. Field and camp relief is stronger, with low siege lanes at the ditch and stone wall. Three winding trampled paths join the forge, campfire, huts and beast yard. Added existing-art anvils, scrap, weapon stalls, carts, cooking pots, butcher racks, cages, supplies, a siege engine and rock outcrops. Random tents form small camp clusters rather than diagonal rows. No new production artwork or asset requests were needed; the concept is an illustration of the direction, not a game screenshot.

The blueprint can declare hidden structural weaknesses. Bloodtusk has rotten timber on the eastern flank and splintered repairs on the southern flank. A living member of a player platoon must come within six tiles of the defence's outside approach with clear line of sight. Discovery emits one report, outlines the structure in amber and enables triple damage through the ordinary assault command. Breaking it opens a usable rubble path. Discovery resets for a new run and survives the existing battle snapshot and worker mirror. Paid pre-assault intelligence is unchanged; scouting preference was asked but not answered, so proximity discovery is the first implementation.

Contour runs follow the actual stencil. Stepped corner tips are grouped with the adjacent run so one breach opens the full thickness; every run is checked to open into the camp at jump 1. Stone-wall volleys and kegs use local wall thickness on the contoured map. Other holds keep their original layout and map-building behaviour. Two existing tests were updated for the approved geometry: short corner runs may contain eight tiles, and a sapper targets the new curtain beside the forge.

## Weathering, storytelling and life

Owner approved all five finishing directions. Intact palisades now vary in height, lean and timber tint, with low broken-rail bracing. Their damage/breach pictures still follow actual health; cosmetic age never opens a fake gap or reveals a weak point. Shallow one-level scallops roughen the summit rim, while paths and foundations retain their stamped heights. Loose scree, small outcrops and tufts soften cliff edges and feet without adding hidden obstacles.

Added a siege repair/assembly yard with spare mechanisms and timber; a captured supply wagon with cargo and discarded shields/banner; an expanded butcher station; and a scorched refuse hollow with bones, broken rails and rubble. Earth tracks connect the work sites and convoy approach, with ruts and muddy wear. A separate trail marker preserves the original stone bridge road. Doorstep wear ties the levelled buildings into their yards.

Four smoke sources and three decorative work sites add quiet activity. Workers currently borrow the existing Orc Grunt figure sheet for hammering/chopping; they have no health rings, targeting or presence in the simulation. They disappear before nearby heroes or the alarm bring combat to the site, and yield to occupied tiles. Pools are bounded by the authored sites (up to three figures and eight puffs). Cloth sways gently; reduced motion holds cloth/workers still and suppresses smoke, while battery saver reduces smoke density. Existing ember/water motion remains unchanged.

Dedicated unarmed labourers, healthy weathered wall variants, an unfinished siege assembly and cliff-edge artwork would improve the borrowed combinations. Their four explicit image prompts are in [the art brief](bloodtusk-weathering-art-prompts.md); those sheets have not been generated or imported. No new runtime assets or URLs were added, and existing credits apply to the reused artwork.
